Every room holds its own air, and air moves through open doors until the rooms match. A shut door stops it, which is what
you want when one side is on fire or open to space, and a nuisance when you only want two rooms to share air.

Crew use up oxygen as they breathe, faster than they add CO₂, so on a ship whose life support stops it is oxygen that runs out.
Five crew on the starter ship with life support off get the low-oxygen alert after about an hour of play, and start to be hurt
about an hour after that.

## Door vents

Every door has a **vent**. Select a door to open its inspector, which names the two sides it joins, shows its state (open, shut, jammed) and whether the air on each side is breathable, and offers **Open vent** (press **Close vent** to seal it again).
With the vent open, air and pressure pass between the two sides even while the door is shut, slower than through an open
door. Fire, heat and smoke do not travel through it.

A vent looks after itself:

- It **shuts on its own** whenever either side has a hazard: fire, smoke, vacuum or thin air, bad air, or dangerous heat or
  cold.
- It needs the ship to have **power** to stay open. With no power it fails shut. It draws no power itself.
- If you left it set to open, it **opens again by itself** once the hazard is gone and power is back.

The door's panel says why a vent you set open is shut: a hazard nearby, or no power. Airlock doors have no vent.

## Walking out without air

Crew without a suit never take a route through vacuum or across an asteroid's bare rock. When their work is out there, like
a turret to repair or an asteroid farm's greenhouse to tend, they put on a suit at an airlock first and take it off again
when they come back in. They take it off only at an airlock that opens on the base's main air, never in a greenhouse that
stands on its own, so they can still walk to the mess and their beds without it.
